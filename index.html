Study 1

Time-boxed, rotating small groups

Implementation intentions make a plan automatic (Gollwitzer, 1999); flexible, rotating grouping is the literature's own antidote to the stigma of visible ability-sorting (Boaler et al., 2000; McGillicuddy & Devine, 2021).

Study 1 · full specification

How every variable is measured

DV — primary

Unmonitored persistence

% of non-CRC-visit days with an observed level-matched small-group session, via unannounced structured observation.

DV — secondary

Learning gain

Pre–post σ change on an ASER-style oral reading / numeracy assessment, matching TaRL's own outcome metric.

DV — tertiary

Stigma / self-concept

Child self-report on an adapted academic self-concept scale, plus observer log of peer commentary at transitions.

Mediator

Habit strength

Adapted Self-Report Habit Index tracking automaticity of initiating the slot without deliberation.

Moderator

Capability exposure

Years of service + NISHTHA-FLN completion — expected to show a weak effect, testing the report's own diagnosis.

Moderator

Rotation transparency

Ability-linked vs. non-ability-linked roster, manipulated as a design arm to test the Belief-4 disruption directly.

Study 2

Peer teacher reinforcement loop

Practice adoption tracks the expertise circulating in a teacher's own network, not just formal training (Frank et al., 2004); sustained, practice-focused peer collaboration shifts instructional norms (Vescio, Ross, & Adams, 2008).

Click "Seed" — a teacher who already groups by level — to trace how a structured peer channel diffuses the practice outward through a cluster of nearby single-teacher schools.

Study 2 · full specification

How every variable is measured

DV — primary

Cluster-level persistence

Proportion of teachers in a 3–5 school cluster above the unmonitored-persistence threshold, tracked one term.

DV — secondary

Diffusion lag

Weeks between one teacher's adoption of a routine and a network-linked teacher's adoption of the same.

Mediator

Network centrality

Sociometric tie-strength measure — who seeks instructional advice from whom — predicting diffusion speed.

Mediator

Self-efficacy

Adapted Teacher Sense of Efficacy Scale, expected to rise via vicarious mastery from peer examples.

Moderator

CRC-mediation

Whether the channel is CRC-run or purely horizontal — expected to weaken the effect if supervisor-mediated.

Moderator

Early-adopter seeding

Presence of one "positive deviant" teacher already practising level-based instruction in the cluster.

Methodology

A complex problem needs a safe-to-fail design

Design notes

  • Design type — multiple-baseline pilot across a small set of school clusters, not a single large RCT.
  • Unit of analysis — Study 1 is teacher/classroom-level; Study 2 is necessarily cluster-level.
  • Falsification check — if capability exposure strongly moderates either effect, it undercuts the report's own COM-B claim.

Limitations

  • No primary data has been collected — effect directions are hypotheses from adjacent (largely Western) literatures.
  • Sociometric measurement is logistically demanding in low-connectivity rural clusters; may need a paper-logbook substitute.
  • Neither design has yet been validated against the report's own personas — Meena and Aarav.
