Literature Review

What the Research Says

1. Learning levels & remediation

Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) (Banerjee et al., 2016)
J-PAL / Pratham · multiple RCTs, 2001–present, India
Pratham's TaRL approach groups children by learning level rather than age or grade, and has been evaluated in six randomized trials across seven Indian states (Banerjee et al., 2016; J-PAL, n.d.). Evaluations report some of the largest effect sizes in the education RCT literature — TaRL learning camps in Uttar Pradesh roughly doubled the share of children who could read a paragraph (J-PAL, n.d.). Training teachers or distributing materials alone did not move outcomes; gains appeared only when teachers had clear goals, child-level progress data, and ongoing mentoring (Banerjee & Duflo, 2015).
Annual Status of Education Report (ASER Centre, 2023)
Pratham · annual, since 2005
Over a decade of household-level testing shows basic literacy and numeracy in Indian elementary schools have stayed persistently low even as enrollment approached universal levels (ASER Centre, 2023; Banerjee & Duflo, 2015) — the same "enrolled but not learning" gap the respondent raised anecdotally.

2. Dropout drivers

Household reasons for dropout (Chugh, 2011)
National University of Educational Planning and Administration (NUEPA)
Large-scale household data consistently rank "has to work for wage/salary," "education not considered useful," and "parents not interested in studies" among the top reasons children leave school (Chugh, 2011) — closely mirroring the respondent's account of daily-wage income outweighing long-term schooling payoff.
Secondary-level dropout as the sharpest drop-off point (Smile Foundation, 2024)
Civil-society sector reporting
India has cut primary-level dropout substantially, but the transition into secondary school remains the largest leak in the pipeline (Smile Foundation, 2024) — consistent with the respondent's observation that dropout concentrates "as soon as they reach the adolescent age."

3. Policy & systems infrastructure

Foundational learning policy (Central Square Foundation, 2021)
India education policy think tank
CSF's work on foundational literacy and numeracy policy (feeding into national programs like NIPUN Bharat) argues that state-level tracking systems and teacher accountability structures — similar to the "Sampurna" enrollment database the respondent described — are necessary but insufficient without instructional reform at the classroom level (Central Square Foundation, 2021).
Independent evaluations of TaRL scale-ups (3ie, 2019)
International Initiative for Impact Evaluation
Independent evaluations, including in Haryana, found TaRL's low marginal cost and government-led delivery model made it politically and fiscally attractive for scale (3ie, 2019) — relevant to any intervention design that has to work within existing public-school staffing and budget constraints.

Gap this review flags

Most rigorous India evidence targets grades 3–5 foundational literacy/numeracy (Banerjee et al., 2016; ASER Centre, 2023). The interview data here concerns a 10th-grade cohort and secondary-level disability accommodation/grace-mark misuse — a stage and mechanism the mainstream TaRL literature does not directly cover. This is the space the discussion and intervention sections need to bridge.
