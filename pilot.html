Abstract

1. Introduction

IMRAD — Introduction

India has achieved near-universal primary enrollment, yet ASER data show approximately half of Grade 5 students cannot read a Grade 2-level text (ASER Centre, 2023). Practitioner accounts corroborate this at the classroom level: screening is typically deferred to Grade 9–10, by which point remediation options narrow to scribes, grace marks, and disability certification — mechanisms designed for genuine need that are increasingly used as exam-passing shortcuts once early deficits go unaddressed. This pilot tests a low-cost repositioning of an already-validated approach (TaRL-style ability grouping, peer tutoring) six to seven years earlier than current practice, using existing teacher time and no new infrastructure.

Hypothesis: Grade 1–3 students flagged as below-level at baseline will show a statistically and practically meaningful shift toward age-appropriate ASER reading/math levels after [X] cycles of Reading Circle participation, relative to [comparison classrooms / historical baseline — specify design].

2.3 Instruments

ASER Reading Tool (Kannada) — 5 levels: Beginner → Letter → Word → Std I text → Std II text. ASER Math Tool — 5 levels: Nothing → 1–9 recognition → 10–99 recognition → Subtraction → Division (ASER Centre, 2018).

2.4 Procedure

  1. Baseline ASER screening, Week 1 of term.
  2. Flagged students (below age-appropriate level) grouped into Reading Circles of 4–5, mixed with 1–2 peer mentors.
  3. 20 min/day scaffolded peer reading practice inside existing school hours.
  4. Re-screening every 6–8 weeks; exit on Pass, escalate to Resource Teacher after 2 cycles of no progress.
  5. Monthly fidelity spot-check by School Head (session occurrence, attendance).

2.5 Analysis Plan

Level-shift (ordinal, 5-point ASER scale) pre vs. post per student; proportion reaching age-appropriate level; fidelity rate (% scheduled sessions actually run) reported alongside outcome data, given documented fidelity failure as the dominant risk at government scale (Duflo et al., 2024).

5.2 Comparison to Prior Evidence

Prior TaRL and peer-tutoring RCTs report effect sizes of 0.28–0.60 SD under NGO-supported conditions, but null or negligible effects under unsupported government-scale delivery, largely attributable to implementation fidelity rather than design (Banerjee et al., 2016; Duflo et al., 2024). This pilot's fidelity metric (§3) is reported explicitly to test whether a no-new-staff model can sustain adequate fidelity absent external monitoring.
